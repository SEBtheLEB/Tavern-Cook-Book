# Tales at a Tavern

## Revised Act I Opening

For two years, Gwen had lived beneath the eye of Tohm Kyatt.

Two years gathering ingredients before sunrise.

Two years learning that a cook who could not hunt, harvest, barter, improvise, and occasionally defend herself from something with very large teeth was not much use outside a kitchen.

Officially, the Academy called it her practical placement.

Gwen called it surviving Tohm.

Those two years in Whisker Woods completed the practical portion of her education, but they did not make her a chef.

Not yet.

For that, Gwen had been required to return to the **Culinary Imperial Academy of Unhold** and face one final examination.

No mentor beside her.

No recipe handed down from Tohm.

No one correcting her seasoning over her shoulder.

She would be given a kitchen, ingredients, a panel of examiners, and one task:

Create a dish worthy of the title of Chef.

So Gwen left Whisker Woods.

She returned to Unhold.

And she cooked.

The dish was entirely hers.

Built from everything the Academy had taught her, everything Tohm had drilled into her, and everything she had learned working among the farms, hunters, fishermen, and strange creatures of Whisker Woods.

When the judges finished tasting it, Gwen stood before them with her hands behind her back and pretended she was not nervous.

Then the head examiner placed the seal before her.

She had passed.

Gwen was officially a chef.

And she had approximately no interest in staying at the Academy to celebrate it.

Because that evening was the **Feast of Full Plates**.

And although Unhold celebrated the Feast on a scale few places in the kingdom could rival—with enormous banquet halls, famous chefs, alumni returning from every corner of the realm, and enough food to feed a small army—Gwen did not want to spend it there.

She wanted to go home.

Not Osul.

Not anymore.

Home was a tavern surrounded by Whisken villagers in the middle of Whisper Woods.

Home was Kap shouting about fish.

Juno picking fights she claimed were training.

Neighbors who had watched Gwen spend two years returning from Tohm's errands covered in mud, slime, feathers, and occasionally something's blood.

And, somewhere inside the tavern, a cat chef who would undoubtedly react to Gwen becoming officially qualified by immediately finding something wrong with her knife technique.

So as soon as the ceremony ended, Gwen packed her things and left Unhold.

She was going back to Whisper Woods.

---

The closer Gwen came to the woods, the stranger things became.

At first she noticed insects.

Too many of them.

Beetles crossing the path in thick little streams.

Dappleflies gathering in clouds where there should have been only a handful.

Egg clusters attached to roots and branches where Gwen had never seen them before.

Whisper Woods had always been alive with bugs.

That was normal.

This wasn't.

Then she heard someone screaming.

"HELP!"

Gwen dropped her travel bag and ran.

The shouting led her toward Kap's pond.

What she found there barely resembled the place she remembered.

The water was dark.

Something oily seemed to drift beneath its surface.

Plants along the bank were dying.

Insects covered the mud.

And Kap was stranded beside the corrupted water with part of his fishing equipment overturned nearby.

"Gwen?!"

"Hello, Kap."

A beetle lunged at her.

Gwen drew her sword.

"Nice to see you too."

She fought through the creatures until the pond began to move.

Something enormous rose from beneath the water.

A Prawnhusk.

Corrupted.

Aggressive.

Far larger than anything Gwen had expected to fight on the journey home from her final exam.

Gwen stared at it.

She had been an officially licensed chef for less than a day.

The Prawnhusk screamed.

Gwen raised her sword.

"Right."

Apparently the celebration could wait.

---

By the time Gwen finally reached the tavern, night had fallen.

Lanterns glowed through the village.

Long tables filled the roads.

Music drifted between buildings.

Children ran beneath strings of colored ribbons while enormous platters moved from kitchen to table faster than Gwen could count them.

The Feast of Full Plates was already underway.

And the moment Gwen stepped into the tavern, the room erupted.

"There she is!"

Someone cheered.

Then everyone did.

Gwen stopped in the doorway.

A banner had been hung crookedly over the main room.

**WELCOME HOME, CHEF GWEN**

Someone had added beneath it:

**AND HAPPY FEAST**

Someone else had added:

**TO EVERYONE, NOT JUST GWEN**

Gwen laughed.

Juno shoved through the crowd first and nearly knocked her over.

"You passed!"

"I told you I would."

"You also told me you weren't nervous."

"I wasn't."

"Liar."

Then came Kap, already telling everyone within earshot that Gwen had somehow managed to rescue him from the pond on the exact same day she became a chef.

The villagers had food waiting.

Ale waiting.

Stories waiting.

For Gwen, the Feast of Full Plates had always meant Whisper Woods.

The holiday celebrated abundance, hospitality, and the old friendship between the Whisken and the creatures of the forest.

No empty plates.

No closed doors.

Food shared freely with neighbor and stranger alike.

And this year, without Gwen ever asking for it, the Feast had become her celebration too.

Then the kitchen doors opened.

Tohm Kyatt walked out.

Gwen smiled.

Tohm looked her up and down.

"You are late."

Gwen's smile disappeared.

"I crossed half the region today."

"Poor planning."

"I also saved Kap."

"That explains Kap."

"And killed a corrupted Prawnhusk."

Tohm paused.

"...That explains the smell."

Gwen stared at him.

Then Tohm's expression softened.

Barely.

He stepped closer.

"So."

He looked at the Academy seal Gwen now wore.

"You passed."

Gwen nodded.

"I passed."

For once, Tohm said nothing clever.

Nothing insulting.

Nothing needlessly dramatic.

He simply smiled.

"My student is a chef."

Gwen felt something tighten unexpectedly in her throat.

Then Tohm clapped his paws together.

"Good. You're working tonight."

The feeling vanished immediately.

"What?"

"The Feast is packed."

"I just arrived."

"Yes."

"I graduated today."

"Yes."

"This is supposed to be my celebration."

"It is."

Tohm gestured toward the kitchen.

"You are celebrating by cooking."

Gwen stared at him.

Tohm stared back.

Then he added:

"And you will make your examination dish."

That stopped her.

"The one I made at Unhold?"

"I have heard enough about it from everyone else."

"You haven't tasted it?"

"I was not invited to your examination."

"You would have yelled at the judges."

"They might have learned something."

Tohm turned toward the kitchen.

"Come."

Gwen followed.

"And Gwen?"

She looked at him.

This time the pride in his face was impossible to hide.

"Make it exactly as you made it there."

He smiled.

"Tonight, Whisper Woods gets to taste what made you a chef."

And so Gwen's first night home was spent exactly as it should have been.

Surrounded by Whisken friends.

Laughing between orders.

Recreating the dish that had earned her title.

Cooking beside Tohm again.

Serving food until there was barely room left on the tables.

For a few hours, Gwen forgot about Kap's pond.

She forgot about the strange insects.

She forgot about the dark water.

Until one of the villagers mentioned that bugs had been appearing everywhere.

Another complained that something had destroyed an entire crop.

Someone else had seen insects moving through the forest in groups.

Then a traveler mentioned frost on the northern road.

Frost.

During a warm season.

The conversations died down.

Tohm stopped chopping for just a fraction of a second.

Gwen noticed.

But before she could ask him anything, another table shouted for food.

The Feast continued.

For tonight, at least.

---

# The Brambrake Gate

Later, as Gwen begins investigating the worsening corruption, her search eventually brings her to a massive gate cutting across part of the forest.

It is ridiculous.

Far too large.

Far too fortified.

And blocking a road Gwen has used countless times.

She knocks.

Nothing.

"Brambrake!"

Silence.

She knocks harder.

"I know you're in there!"

Nothing.

Gwen waits.

Eventually a voice comes from somewhere beyond the gate.

"Go away."

Gwen looks around.

She cannot see anyone.

"Brambrake?"

"No."

"That is very obviously you."

"No, it isn't."

"I've spoken to you before."

"Then you should recognize when I don't want to speak to you now."

Gwen tries looking through gaps in the structure.

Nothing.

No face.

No silhouette.

Brambrake has built the thing so thoroughly that Gwen cannot even tell exactly where he is standing.

"There's something happening in the woods."

"Not in here."

"Bugs are attacking people."

"Not in here."

"Kap's pond is corrupted."

"Kap should move."

"Brambrake."

Silence.

"I'm investigating the northern woods."

"No."

"That's not actually your decision."

"My gate."

"Not your forest."

"My side of the gate."

"You can't claim half of Whisper Woods by putting a door in front of it."

"I appear to have done exactly that."

Gwen stares at the enormous gate.

She still never sees him.

Eventually the voice disappears.

The conversation is over.

For now.

Brambrake remains nothing more than a voice behind wood and stone.

Gwen does not properly see him until much later, when she finally earns—or forces—her way through and Brambrake himself allows her inside.

---

# The Bug Misunderstanding

By this point, insects have become the obvious pattern behind almost everything going wrong.

Kap's pond is overrun.

Eggs are appearing throughout the woods.

Beetles are attacking travelers.

Strange nests are forming.

Rumors are spreading about an Ant Queen.

So when Gwen begins hearing villagers talking about **Bug**, she assumes the obvious.

Another insect.

Except this one sounds considerably worse.

"I saw Bug near the western trail."

"Bug hit the miller."

"Bug's throwing lightning again."

"Bug stole someone's bag."

Gwen stops.

"Wait."

The villager nods grimly.

"Terrible business."

Gwen grips her sword.

"So now they're using electricity."

"What?"

"The bugs."

"What bugs?"

"The bugs!"

The villager looks confused.

Gwen leaves before the conversation becomes useful.

Soon reports get worse.

Someone claims Bug blasted a tree apart.

Another person saw blue lightning between the branches.

A farmer swears Bug laughed at him.

Gwen begins imagining some horrifying new corrupted insect species.

Possibly something produced by the same magical contamination affecting the rest of the forest.

And then she finally corners it.

Lightning flashes through the trees.

Gwen draws her sword.

"Got you."

Something small moves behind a rock.

She rushes forward.

"All right, you horrible little—"

A goblin jumps out.

Gwen stops.

The goblin stops.

They stare at each other.

He is short.

Green.

Wearing mismatched equipment.

Electricity crackles between his fingers.

Gwen slowly lowers her sword.

"...You're a goblin."

The goblin looks offended.

"Yeah."

"Are you Bug?"

"Yeah."

Gwen looks around.

Then back at him.

"Your name is Bug?"

"Yeah."

"You're not a bug."

Bug squints at her.

"No."

Gwen closes her eyes.

For days she has been tracking an imaginary lightning beetle.

Bug raises one hand.

Lightning jumps between his fingers.

"So are we fighting or what?"

Gwen opens her eyes.

"Give me a second."

"What?"

"I'm reevaluating several conversations."

Bug attacks.

And that is how Gwen meets the goblin named Bug.

The misunderstanding becomes funny in retrospect, but it also matters.

Because Bug's lightning magic is real.

And his presence near the northern woods is not random.

His story becomes another small thread leading Gwen toward what is actually happening beyond Brambrake's gate—and toward the much larger secret spreading beneath Whisper Woods.
