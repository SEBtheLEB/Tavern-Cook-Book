# The Merchant Beneath the Earth

## The Origin of the Masil Cult

### Chapter I: A Man With Something to Sell

Long before anyone bowed their head at the name of Masil, before the word **FEAST** was painted across banners or whispered behind locked doors, the man who would lead the cult was little more than a traveling merchant.

He had no kingdom, no inheritance, and no great craft that distinguished him from the hundreds of traders who wandered from village to village. Everything he owned traveled with him. Bolts of cloth. Dried herbs. Cheap jewelry polished until it could almost pass for silver. Spices bought in one town and sold for twice their worth in the next. Little charms whose origins changed depending on who was asking.

He was good at selling things.

More importantly, he was good at selling stories.

A necklace was never simply a necklace. It had belonged to a sailor who survived an impossible storm.

A bottle of tonic was never simply bitter roots soaked in alcohol. It had been brewed according to a recipe known only to healers beyond the mountains.

A dull little stone became a blessing from an Ovenhold pilgrim if the customer looked particularly devout.

He learned quickly that people rarely paid for the object in his hand.

They paid for what they wanted the object to mean.

For years, he lived comfortably enough this way. He ate in taverns, slept beneath roofs when business was good, and beneath his cart when it was not. He crossed paths with farmers, soldiers, nobles, thieves, priests, cooks, and pilgrims. He listened carefully to all of them.

Everywhere he went, he heard the teachings of the Triad.

**Love. Taste. Passion.**

He saw the symbols carved above kitchens. Heard blessings spoken before shared meals. Watched families leave portions of food for neighbors who had less. He listened to travelers tell the story of the Tablemaker and the meal that brought two starving peoples away from annihilation.

The merchant understood its importance.

But he did not possess the reverence others seemed to have for it.

To him, faith was another thing people carried with them.

And anything people carried could be sold back to them.

Still, he was not yet a preacher.

He had no interest in changing the world.

He wanted what he had always wanted.

A warm bed.

A heavy purse.

Good food.

Beautiful company.

And enough admiration that he would never again have to stand in a marketplace begging strangers to stop at his stall.

He wanted to enter a room and have people already know his name.

For a long time, that desire remained only a fantasy.

Then his fortunes began to change.

### Chapter II: An Empty Cart

It happened slowly.

One bad road became three.

A wagon wheel split and ruined nearly a quarter of his goods.

A shipment of spice spoiled after rain seeped beneath its covering.

In another town, a merchant with better merchandise arrived only a day before him and undercut nearly every price he could offer.

Then came the theft.

He awoke one morning beside the remains of a small fire and found one of his packs gone.

Coins, jewelry, and several of his most valuable items had disappeared with it.

By the time he reached the next settlement, the prosperous merchant he pretended to be had been reduced to a man arranging scraps on a wooden table.

People walked past him.

Some looked.

Few bought.

For perhaps the first time in his life, his stories were no longer enough.

He began borrowing.

Then lying about when he could repay what he borrowed.

Then avoiding the towns where those promises had been made.

The road became less romantic with an empty purse.

He ate less.

He slept outside more often.

His clothes grew worn, and the charming appearance he had once carefully maintained began to resemble exactly what he feared becoming.

Common.

Forgettable.

Poor.

He hated it.

He watched successful merchants pass him with full wagons. He watched pilgrims receive free meals from families simply because they carried a holy emblem around their necks. He watched respected teachers enter villages and immediately receive an audience while he struggled to convince anyone to purchase a handful of dried berries.

Respect, he realized, was worth more than merchandise.

A man with goods had to convince people to listen.

A man believed to possess truth only had to begin speaking.

The thought remained with him.

### Chapter III: The Hole

One evening, far from any town he knew well, the merchant left the road.

He had been told that another settlement lay beyond the hills, though he had begun to suspect the directions were wrong. The sun descended behind him, and the path narrowed until even the wheels of his cart could no longer pass comfortably.

Eventually, he abandoned it beneath a tree and continued on foot.

He carried a small lantern, a half-empty water skin, and what little food remained.

There was still daylight when he found the hole.

At first, he nearly walked past it.

It was hidden among tall grass and tangled roots, an unnatural opening in the earth wide enough for a man to climb through. Stones disappeared into the darkness below. A faint draft rose from somewhere beneath the ground.

He crouched beside it.

There was something strange about the air coming from below.

Warm.

Sweet.

Almost fragrant.

He lowered his lantern.

The light revealed a steep descent of soil and ancient stone.

He should have left.

Instead, he thought of treasure.

Old ruins.

Buried offerings.

Something forgotten.

Something valuable.

For a merchant with almost nothing left to sell, that possibility was enough.

He tied a rope around a nearby tree and began climbing.

The descent was deeper than he expected.

Roots hung from the ceiling above him. Dirt crumbled beneath his boots. His lantern cast long shadows across stone that had not seen daylight in years.

At the bottom, the narrow opening widened.

The merchant froze.

He was no longer standing in a simple pit.

There was space beneath the earth.

A chamber formed by stone and soil, almost perfectly hidden from the world above.

And somewhere within that darkness, he heard a woman singing.

He nearly dropped the lantern.

The song was quiet.

Beautiful.

He could not understand the words.

But he understood the feeling they placed inside him.

Longing.

He followed it.

### Chapter IV: The Woman Below

The chamber opened before him.

There, beneath the earth, sat a woman.

For several moments, the merchant could do nothing but stare.

She was beautiful beyond anyone he had ever seen.

Her clothing was unlike anything sold in the markets he knew. Her hair fell around her with impossible grace, untouched by the dirt and damp that surrounded them. Even the weak light from his lantern seemed to gather around her.

Then he saw the wings.

The merchant fell to his knees.

Every story he had ever heard suddenly seemed inadequate.

He knew what stood before him.

An angel.

A messenger.

Something that belonged to the heavens rather than a hole beneath the ground.

The woman looked at him.

She smiled.

And she spoke his name.

He had never told her.

Fear passed through him, followed almost immediately by something stronger.

Wonder.

She told him that his suffering had been seen.

His humiliation had been seen.

His hunger had been seen.

The merchant listened without moving.

She spoke of a world filled with people who denied themselves what they desired and called that denial virtue.

They hungered, yet restrained themselves.

They desired, yet felt shame.

They possessed abundance, yet were taught to surrender it.

They tasted pleasure and immediately searched for reasons to feel guilty.

Then she spoke of the Triad.

Love.

Taste.

Passion.

The words were familiar.

But the way she spoke them was not.

She told him humanity had misunderstood what it had been given.

Why should love be limited?

Why should taste be restrained?

Why should passion ever be silenced?

What good was a table overflowing with food if a man stopped eating while hunger for pleasure still remained inside him?

What good was desire if it had been placed within the heart only to be resisted?

The merchant listened.

The angel drew closer.

She told him there was another teaching hidden beneath the timid interpretations of priests and scholars.

A teaching for those willing to accept the fullness of what had been given to them.

She gave it a name.

**FEAST.**

Not merely a meal.

A way of living.

To take deeply from everything the world offered.

Food.

Drink.

Gold.

Beauty.

Pleasure.

Admiration.

Power.

To reject shame for wanting more.

To stop apologizing for appetite.

To feast upon life itself.

The merchant had spent years listening to people speak about sacrifice.

For the first time, something holy appeared to be telling him the opposite.

And more importantly, it was telling him exactly what he wanted to hear.

### Chapter V: The First Verses

The angel began to speak again.

This time, the merchant reached into his pack.

He pulled out an old sales ledger and opened it to the first empty page he could find.

He wrote.

Every sentence she gave him.

Every strange proverb.

Every promise.

Every command.

The merchant who had once invented histories for necklaces and medicines now sat trembling in the dirt, recording words spoken by something he believed had come from Heaven itself.

Some passages seemed beautiful.

Some unsettled him.

Some he did not entirely understand.

But all of them sounded powerful.

And the merchant understood power when he heard it.

Then the angel told him something that changed the course of his life.

These words were not meant for him alone.

They were to be carried upward.

Shared.

Taught.

Spread from table to table and mouth to mouth.

For a moment, the merchant felt afraid.

He was no priest.

No scholar.

No noble.

No prophet.

He was a salesman with an empty cart.

But then another thought entered his mind.

People would listen to a man chosen by an angel.

He imagined entering a village and no longer needing to shout prices from behind a wooden stall.

He imagined people approaching *him*.

He imagined invitations into wealthy homes.

Gifts.

Fine clothes.

Wine.

Rooms prepared for his arrival.

Crowds gathered around him.

Women whispering his name.

Men seeking his approval.

He imagined never being ignored again.

The angel asked whether he would carry her words into the world.

The merchant bowed his head.

He said yes.

And somewhere behind the beauty of her smile, something ancient was pleased.

### Chapter VI: The Prophet on the Road

When the merchant climbed from the hole, the world above looked exactly as he had left it.

The same grass.

The same trees.

The same fading evening sky.

But he no longer saw himself as the same man who had climbed down.

His cart was still nearly empty.

His purse was still light.

His clothes were still worn.

Yet inside his pack rested something more valuable than anything he had ever carried between towns.

A message.

He began small.

At the next settlement, he told a tavern keeper that he had encountered an angel beneath the earth.

The man laughed.

So the merchant showed him the verses.

He did what he had always done.

He told a story.

Except this time, he did not need to invent the most extraordinary part.

A few people listened.

Then more.

He spoke of appetite without shame.

Of pleasure without apology.

Of a world where abundance itself was sacred.

He borrowed familiar language from the Triad and reshaped it around the angel's words.

Love became the right to pursue whatever the heart desired.

Taste became the duty to experience everything available to the senses.

Passion became justification for refusing restraint.

And above them all stood the word she had given him.

**FEAST.**

People who already wanted permission to indulge found comfort in him.

People angry at rules found freedom in him.

People who were lonely found community around his tables.

People who were wealthy discovered a doctrine that declared their excess a virtue.

And wherever the merchant traveled, the gatherings grew larger.

Soon, people stopped calling him a merchant.

They began asking him questions.

They asked what the angel wanted.

They asked how they should live.

They asked whether certain pleasures were permitted.

He always had an answer.

Sometimes the answer came from the verses.

Sometimes it came from his own mouth.

The distinction became easier to ignore with every passing week.

Gifts followed.

Food.

Coins.

Clothing.

Places to sleep.

Exactly as he had imagined.

The man who once chased customers through marketplaces now watched people compete for the honor of hosting him.

For the first time in his life, people wanted what only he could provide.

Access to the words beneath the earth.

### Chapter VII: The First Feast

Eventually, his followers stopped fitting around a single tavern table.

They gathered in halls.

Barns.

Cellars.

Private estates.

Great meals became central to the gatherings.

Nothing was to be modest.

Nothing restrained.

Plates were filled beyond need.

Cups refilled before they were empty.

Music grew louder.

Pleasures that once would have been hidden were celebrated openly.

At the head of the table sat the merchant.

His followers asked what they should call themselves.

He looked across the feast before him.

At the food.

At the gold.

At the faces waiting eagerly for his answer.

Years earlier, he would have sold every object in that room if given the opportunity.

Now the entire room belonged to him without purchasing any of it.

He thought of the woman beneath the ground.

He thought of her wings.

Her voice.

Her verses.

Her promise of a life without shame for wanting more.

Then he raised his cup.

And he gave the movement a name.

**Masil.**

The people around him repeated it.

Once.

Then again.

Louder.

**Masil.**

The traveling merchant had finally become what he had always secretly wished to be.

A man whose arrival mattered.

A man people fed.

A man people paid.

A man people desired to stand near.

A man whose words could change the behavior of an entire room.

He called himself the servant of the angel and the teacher of FEAST.

His followers called him their leader.

And beneath the earth, hidden from every one of them, the beautiful angel waited.

The merchant believed he had discovered the opportunity that would make him rich.

His followers believed they had discovered a forgotten divine truth.

Neither understood that something far older had just found its first open door into the world.
