# Tales at a Tavern

## Act I — The Queen Beneath the Frost

There had always been bugs in Whisker Woods.

That was what everyone would tell Gwen later.

Beetles beneath fallen logs. Dappleflies dancing over the ponds. Crayhusks burrowing into the wet banks. Ants beneath the roots. Creatures that crawled, buzzed, clicked, and hummed through a forest so fertile that almost anything planted in its soil seemed determined to grow.

The bugs were part of Whisker Woods.

Once, they had even been its friends.

But when Gwen returned to the woods, the first thing she noticed was how quiet the birds had become.

And the second was how loud the insects were.

Gwen was twenty-three now, though in some ways Whisker Woods still made her feel like the girl who had first caught the attention of Tohm Kyatt years ago—the absurdly young student from Osul who had entered the Imperial Culinary Academy of Ovenhold before most students her age were even allowed to apply.

They had called her **the Young Flame** back then.

She hated the name.

Tohm loved it.

For two years he had trained her harder than anyone else. Not simply in cooking. In tracking. Gathering. Hunting. Knowing whether a berry had ripened from its smell before plucking it. Knowing which part of an animal belonged in a stew and which belonged over flame. Knowing that a chef who could not find food without a market was only half a chef.

She had finished at the top of his class.

And now she was coming back to Whisker Woods to work beside him.

She imagined her return would begin with Tohm criticizing how she held a knife.

Instead, it began with an insect nest blocking the road.

Gwen stopped.

Eggs covered the roots ahead.

Dozens of them.

Maybe hundreds.

Pale, swollen things clung beneath branches and between stones. Some were freshly laid. Others had already split open.

Gwen stared at them.

Crayhusks nested near water.

They did **not** lay their eggs on the surface like this.

And they certainly did not lay this many.

Something clicked behind her.

Gwen slowly reached for her sword.

Then another click answered it.

Then another.

The forest erupted.

The first creatures were small enough that Gwen dispatched them quickly, but there were far too many. Beetles rushed from the brush. Dappleflies dove at her face. A Crayhusk snapped at her boots.

Gwen fought her way through them, smashed the eggs blocking the trail, and kept moving.

That was when she heard the scream.

"HELP!"

Gwen froze.

Another scream echoed from somewhere below the trail.

"ANYONE!"

She ran.

The path descended toward a pond.

Or what had once been a pond.

The water was wrong.

Dark threads drifted beneath its surface like ink suspended in glass. Dead reeds bent toward the center. Insects covered the banks.

And half-submerged near the water was a wrecked carriage.

A Whisken fisherman was trapped beside it.

Kap.

Gwen barely had time to recognize him before the insects descended again.

She threw herself between Kap and the swarm.

By the time the last Dapplefly hit the mud, Gwen was breathing hard.

Kap pointed at the pond.

"Gwen."

The water bulged.

Something enormous rose from beneath it.

A Prawnhusk.

Its shell was streaked with the same sickly corruption that stained the water. One claw was nearly the size of Gwen.

She stared up at it.

"Oh, come on."

The Prawnhusk screamed.

Gwen drew her sword.

And her first real fight in Whisker Woods began.

---

By the time Gwen dragged Kap back toward the village, she had mud in her hair, a tear in one sleeve, three cracked healing ales in her basket, and a new appreciation for just how unpleasant something could become when given six legs and a bad attitude.

Kap's carriage was ruined.

His pond was corrupted.

And neither of them knew why.

Worse still, insects had never behaved that way toward the Whisken.

Not like this.

The old stories claimed that when the Whisken first came to these woods after losing their home, they had encountered an ancient conflict beneath the forest.

A Mad Ant King had ruled through hunger.

He devoured everything his colony gathered, starving even his own people. When the Whisken arrived, they helped the Ant Queen overthrow him and seal him away.

In gratitude, the Queen welcomed the Whisken into her forest.

Some stories even claimed it was she who had first called the land **Whisker Woods**.

The truth had become tangled with legend over generations.

But the friendship remained.

The Feast of Full Plates celebrated that friendship.

Food shared rather than hoarded.

Harvest given rather than consumed alone.

Whisken tables filled until no guest could leave hungry.

And now, days before the celebration, the insects were attacking people.

Kap looked back toward his pond.

"Something's frightened them."

Gwen looked at the dead bugs scattered along the trail.

"No."

She had fought frightened animals before.

"Something's commanding them."

---

The village should have made Gwen forget about it.

Almost did.

Lanterns hung from windows. Banners crossed the roads. Huge tables were being assembled for the Feast of Full Plates. Kitchens smoked from morning until night.

And above it all stood the tavern.

Tohm Kyatt's tavern.

Gwen had barely opened the door when something furry struck her in the chest.

"You're late."

Gwen stumbled backward.

Tohm Kyatt stood before her, arms folded.

The world-renowned Whisken chef.

Legendary critic.

Her former academy mentor.

And apparently still incapable of saying hello like a normal person.

"I just killed something the size of your kitchen."

"Excuses."

"Kap almost died."

"Better excuse."

"His pond is corrupted."

Tohm's expression changed.

Only for a moment.

But Gwen saw it.

Concern.

Or recognition.

Then it vanished beneath the smile of a chef with twenty things cooking at once.

"Tell me while you work."

And just like that, Gwen was home.

For the next several hours she chopped, stirred, hauled crates, cleaned tables, prepared sauces and listened to Tohm insist that everything was under control.

It clearly was not.

Ingredients were arriving late because roads had become unsafe.

Farmers complained that insects were eating entire beds of vegetables.

The southern groves had gone strangely cold at night.

Someone had found ice beneath a tree.

In spring.

And Tohm had a list for Gwen.

Of course he did.

Two portions of boar meat.

Thirteen Purfox.

Four Sunchee.

Turnips.

Specialty herbs.

And whatever usable slime gel she could collect.

Gwen stared at the parchment.

"I've been here six hours."

"Then you have had six hours to become rested."

"That's not how rest works."

"Perhaps in Osul."

---

The gathering trips became Gwen's first real rhythm in Whisker Woods.

Leave the tavern.

Fill the basket.

Return alive.

At first, they were almost peaceful.

She dug turnips from the softer fields beyond the village.

She harvested Sunchee where the morning light hit the hill.

She searched for Purfox among thorny shrubs.

She learned which trees still produced good fruit despite the strange changes spreading across the forest.

And she hunted slimes.

Slimes were strange little accidents of abundance.

Where Whisker Woods produced more nutrients than plants could absorb, sometimes those nutrients gathered together and became something almost alive.

Strawberry slimes smelled faintly sweet.

Blueberry slimes stained Gwen's gloves purple.

Their gel could substitute for certain fruit ingredients.

More importantly, Tohm showed Gwen how to brew it.

Ale.

Tonics.

Healing draughts.

Small things.

Practical things.

Food did not always need to change the world.

Sometimes it simply needed to stop you from bleeding.

Snacks worked much the same way.

A crushed Purfox mixed with honey and Sunchee produced a sharp little battle snack that sent warmth through Gwen's arms and made every swing land harder.

When a wall of strange frozen brush blocked one of the village roads, Gwen ate one, felt strength rush into her shoulders, and shattered through.

She looked at the ice afterward.

It did not melt.

That bothered her.

The insects bothered her more.

Every day there were more.

---

Among the villagers Gwen checked on was Bramrik.

Bramrik had decided that the best response to everyone else's problems was to build a gate.

A very large gate.

Across an entire section of Whisker Woods.

Gwen stood outside it one afternoon.

"Bramrik!"

Nothing.

She knocked harder.

"Bramrik!"

A face appeared above the gate.

"What?"

"I'm checking on you."

"I'm alive."

"I can see that."

"Then go away."

Bramrik had always possessed the warmth of a wet stone.

He hated noise.

He hated company.

He hated unexpected visitors.

He hated expected visitors slightly more because they had time to reconsider and came anyway.

Recently, he had apparently decided he also owned several acres of Whisker Woods.

"My side doesn't have bugs," he told Gwen.

"Your side?"

"My side."

"You don't own the woods."

"I put up a gate."

"That isn't how property works."

"Works well enough."

Gwen leaned against it.

"Bugs are getting worse."

"No bugs here."

"Yet."

"Not my problem."

"It becomes your problem when an army of ants eats your house."

Bramrik disappeared from view.

Gwen shouted after him.

"Nobody owns Whisker Woods!"

His voice came back from somewhere inside.

"I do!"

Gwen grinned.

"Pretty sure the Ant Queen would disagree!"

Silence.

"Actually," Gwen added, "she'd probably have the decency not to claim it."

Something struck the gate from the other side.

Gwen decided to leave.

She checked on him again the next day.

And the day after that.

Bramrik hated that too.

---

The Feast of Full Plates arrived beneath a sky full of lanterns.

For one evening, Whisker Woods seemed normal again.

Tables covered the village roads.

Ale flowed.

Children chased each other between stalls.

Farmers traded vegetables for pastries.

Hunters brought meat directly to outdoor grills.

Gwen spent most of the celebration behind Tohm's kitchen counter.

And that was where she saw Ressa Vale.

Gwen stopped chopping.

Ressa smiled.

"Oh."

Gwen sighed.

"Oh."

Years earlier at the Imperial Culinary Academy of Ovenhold, Ressa had been one of the few students who could consistently irritate Gwen enough to improve her cooking.

Ressa believed food was precision.

Gwen believed food was instinct disciplined by technique.

Ressa measured everything.

Gwen occasionally measured things by looking at Tohm and asking whether he seemed disappointed yet.

They had spent years competing.

Grades.

Ingredients.

Knife work.

Field assignments.

And, occasionally, Tohm's approval.

Ressa looked around the tavern.

"So this is where the Young Flame ended up."

"Call me that again and I'll put you in the stew."

Ressa smiled wider.

Ten minutes later they were standing opposite each other at two cooking stations.

The villagers gathered around them.

Tohm made absolutely no attempt to stop it.

In fact, Gwen suspected he had arranged the entire thing.

The Cook Battle began.

Each round mattered.

Choose ingredients.

Prepare.

Season.

Control heat.

Commit.

Ressa built her dish carefully, protecting her Technique score and constructing clean flavors one layer at a time.

Gwen took risks.

A little more acidity.

A harder sear.

A local slime reduction Ressa had dismissed as peasant substitution.

Each turn gave them a choice.

Improve the dish.

Recover from a mistake.

Pressure the opponent.

Protect an ingredient.

Push the heat.

Take a risk for Taste.

Sacrifice Presentation for flavor.

Ressa tried to rattle Gwen.

Gwen retaliated by loudly asking whether Ressa still labeled her spice jars alphabetically.

"I like organization."

"You terrify me."

When the dishes finally reached the table, the judges scored them on Taste, Technique and Presentation.

Gwen won.

Barely.

Ressa stared at the result.

Then tasted Gwen's sauce again.

"The slime worked."

Gwen smiled.

"The slime worked."

Ressa pointed her spoon at her.

"I still hate you."

"No you don't."

"Professionally."

That night, over drinks, Ressa mentioned something strange.

Her caravan had crossed the northern road.

Half her produce had frozen solid.

Not chilled.

Frozen.

One of her guards had also seen thousands of insects moving north after sundown.

Not feeding.

Marching.

That wiped the smile from Gwen's face.

---

Later that night, Tohm placed a covered dish in front of Gwen.

"This," he said, "is different."

Gwen looked at him.

Different was not a word Tohm used lightly.

Under the lid was a meal unlike the little snacks and broths she had been making.

Its aroma alone carried heat.

Not spice.

Heat.

The air above it shimmered.

Gwen stared.

"What is it?"

"The Fire Meal."

Tohm had spent days sending her through Whisker Woods for its ingredients.

Turnips pulled from mineral-rich soil.

Sunchee gathered at their brightest.

Rare herbs.

Meat.

Spices.

Ingredients Gwen had fought bugs, slimes and boars to bring home.

But preparing them together changed something.

The meal felt alive.

Tohm watched her carefully.

"Meals like this are not snacks, Gwen."

"You've mentioned that."

"They do not merely strengthen you."

He tapped his temple.

"They leave something behind."

Gwen raised a fork.

"You could have said that before I agreed to eat it."

"I am saying it before you eat it."

"By approximately four seconds."

Gwen took the bite.

For one heartbeat, nothing happened.

Then the tavern disappeared.

---

Snow.

Gwen opened her eyes in a blizzard.

She was standing in Whisker Woods.

But it was spring.

She knew it was spring.

She had walked these paths hours ago beneath green leaves.

Now every branch was white.

Ice covered the roots.

Wind screamed between the trees.

"Gwen!"

She turned.

Nothing.

The voice disappeared into the storm.

She walked.

Her hands were freezing.

Her sword felt heavier with every step.

Eventually she found a cave and stumbled inside.

Darkness swallowed her.

Gwen searched her pack.

Flint.

Torch.

She struck a spark.

Fire burst to life.

And something in the cave moved.

Click.

Click-click.

Click-click-click-click.

Gwen lifted the torch.

The ceiling moved.

Bugs.

Hundreds.

No.

Thousands.

Eggs covered the walls.

Crayhusks crawled between frozen roots.

Beetles poured from cracks.

Dappleflies clung together in black masses.

The nest went deeper than anything Gwen had seen near Kap's pond.

And every creature carried frost.

One charged.

Gwen reached for the Fire Meal.

She did not know why it was there.

She simply knew what to do.

She ate.

Flame erupted around her sword.

Heat exploded through the cavern.

For a moment Gwen was no longer cold.

She was the warmest thing in the world.

She tore through the swarm.

Fire raced along the frozen ground.

A slash became a burning arc.

A thrust sent flame through three insects at once.

Then, with strength she did not understand, Gwen drove her sword downward.

Something like a falling star struck the cavern.

The horde vanished beneath fire.

Silence returned.

Gwen stood breathing amid smoke.

Then she heard singing.

A child.

Soft.

Somewhere deeper inside.

Gwen followed it.

The cavern widened.

The firelight barely reached the far wall.

A girl was kneeling in the darkness.

Small.

Young.

Purple nightgown.

A little stuffed faery clutched beneath one arm.

She was tending to something enormous.

Gwen stepped closer.

The creature moved.

A leg shifted.

Then another.

Chitin.

A cracked shell.

An insect.

A very large insect.

But the shadows hid its shape.

The girl dipped cloth into a bowl and pressed it gently against a wound along the creature's body.

The enormous bug trembled.

"It's all right," the girl whispered.

She began humming again.

Not afraid of it.

Comforting it.

Gwen took another step.

"Hey."

The girl did not react.

"Can you hear me?"

Nothing.

Gwen moved around them, trying to see her face.

But every angle seemed wrong.

Shadow swallowed the girl's features.

Shadow swallowed the insect's head.

Like the memory itself refused to show her.

The girl placed something beside the creature.

Food.

Water.

Then rested her tiny hand against its shell.

"You'll be all right."

Voices echoed somewhere outside.

Men.

Searching.

"Princess!"

The girl looked toward the cave entrance.

Gwen froze.

Princess?

The creature raised one trembling limb.

The girl smiled at it.

Then everything went black.

---

Gwen woke on the tavern floor.

Juno was kneeling over her.

"She's alive."

"Obviously she's alive," Tohm snapped.

"You looked worried."

"I was evaluating."

"You were yelling."

"I evaluate loudly."

Gwen sat up.

Heat rolled from her skin.

A scorched circle surrounded her.

"What happened?"

Tohm did not answer immediately.

Gwen looked at him.

"I saw Whisker Woods."

Still nothing.

"Frozen."

That got his attention.

"There were bugs everywhere."

Tohm's ears lowered.

"And there was a girl."

Something in his face changed.

"A child," Gwen continued. "Purple nightgown. She had one of those stuffed faery dolls. She was helping something. Some enormous bug, I think."

Tohm looked away.

"Someone called her Princess."

Silence.

"Tohm."

"You ate magical food," he said quietly. "You experienced something magical."

"Excellent explanation."

"It is the explanation you are receiving tonight."

Gwen stared at him.

Juno stared at him too.

Tohm left the room.

And for the first time since Gwen had known him, she saw her mentor walk away from a question because he was afraid of the answer.

---

The next morning, Juno threw a wooden practice sword at Gwen.

Gwen caught it.

"No."

"Yes."

"I almost exploded yesterday."

"Exactly."

Juno had been Gwen's friend long enough to know when sympathy would annoy her.

They had trained together under Lel Kai.

They had fought together.

Argued together.

Lost together.

And Juno knew better than anyone that giving Gwen supernatural fire and hoping she would figure it out herself was a terrible plan.

So they sparred.

At first it was familiar.

Block.

Strike.

Dodge.

Counter.

Then Gwen activated the Fire Meal.

Her sword ignited.

Juno stopped.

"That feels unfair."

"You suggested this."

"I've reconsidered."

The friendly spar became Gwen's first true lesson in magical combat.

Fire could increase the reach of an attack.

It could punish an enemy for staying close.

It could turn a heavy strike into an explosion.

With enough energy, Gwen could even call down the devastating fire attack she had used inside the trance.

But power made her reckless.

Juno exploited that immediately.

Every time Gwen chased damage, Juno punished her.

Every time Gwen forgot her footing, Juno swept her legs.

Every time Gwen treated the meal like a replacement for fighting well, Juno reminded her why Lel Kai had trained them for years before either of them could throw fire.

Eventually Gwen stopped trying to overpower her.

She waited.

Blocked.

Moved.

Forced Juno into the position she wanted.

Then struck.

Juno hit the grass.

Silence.

Gwen lowered the practice sword.

Juno stared up at her.

"...I hate magical cooking."

Gwen offered her a hand.

"No you don't."

Juno took it.

"Professionally."

---

After the Fire Meal, the world opened.

Not physically.

Gwen simply began seeing possibilities everywhere.

A slime was no longer just an enemy.

It was an ale ingredient.

A bitter herb could become medicine.

A Purfox could become a damage snack.

A meal could alter the way Gwen fought entirely.

Food became part of every expedition.

Before leaving the tavern, Gwen planned.

Healing ales.

Quick snacks.

Fire Meal portions.

Ingredients she could prepare at camp.

Every trip became a conversation between the kitchen and the sword.

And every trip revealed something worse happening to Whisker Woods.

The next major sign was the boar.

Villagers had started whispering about an enormous creature in the western grove.

Gwen expected exaggeration.

It was not exaggeration.

The boar stood nearly twice the height it should have.

Strange magic pulsed beneath its hide.

Plants around its den grew in spirals.

Its breath steamed in the afternoon air.

When it charged, Gwen barely escaped.

The fight tore through the grove.

Gwen burned through snacks, drank an ale while sprinting sideways, and finally used the Fire Meal to bring the creature down.

Inside its den she found traces of something stranger than natural magic.

Residue.

The same kind of energy she felt when eating Tohm's meals.

Someone—or something—had been feeding magical food into Whisker Woods.

Before Gwen could examine it properly, a tiny insect dropped from a branch.

It seized one of the magical fragments.

Gwen stared at it.

The bug stared back.

"No."

The bug ran.

"NO."

Gwen chased it halfway across the woods.

She lost it beneath the roots.

---

Two days later she saw it again.

It had changed.

Lightning crawled across its shell.

Every few seconds the insect discharged enough electricity to blacken the grass beneath it.

Apparently stealing unknown magical ingredients and eating them was unwise even for bugs.

Gwen followed it.

The creature fled north.

Past Bramrik's gate.

Or rather, toward it.

Bramrik was already waiting.

"No."

"I haven't asked yet."

"No."

"The bug went through your side."

"No bugs."

"I watched it go under your fence."

"Different bug."

"Bramrik."

"No."

So Gwen did what years under Tohm Kyatt had trained her to do.

She ignored him and solved the problem herself.

Near the gate stood three old rotating stone pillars.

Each carried ancient symbols.

Nearby, faded ribbons had been tied around the branches.

Red.

Green.

Blue.

A weathered carving depicted a cauldron beneath three marks.

Passion.

Taste.

Love.

The old principles appeared throughout Whisken culinary tradition.

On a damaged plaque Gwen could still read:

**First, Passion.
Then, Taste.
Lastly, Love.**

She rotated the pillars.

Red.

Green.

Blue.

The symbols illuminated.

Somewhere beneath them, ancient mechanisms groaned.

Bramrik stared.

The gate opened.

Gwen looked at him.

"You put a puzzle on your gate?"

"I didn't build that part."

"Then this isn't your gate."

"It is on my side."

"Nobody owns Whisker Woods."

Bramrik pointed beyond it.

"Go get eaten."

"Thank you."

And Gwen entered the northern woods.

Bramrik's claim that there were no bugs lasted approximately six minutes.

---

The lightning bug led her to a cavern entrance.

Something guarded it.

At first Gwen thought it was a pile of bones.

Then the bones stood.

Thairrott.

A massive skeletal beast dragged itself from the darkness, roots woven between its ribs, skull crowned in broken branches.

The bugs avoided it.

That told Gwen enough.

Whatever lay beneath the forest had acquired guardians.

Thairrott attacked.

The battle became less a duel and more an attempt to survive being struck by an angry building.

Gwen burned through Fire Meal energy breaking corrupted roots from its joints.

She rolled beneath claws.

Used the cavern walls against it.

Drank the last of her ale.

And finally shattered the growth holding its spine together.

Thairrott collapsed.

The entrance opened.

Cold air poured from below.

Gwen looked into the darkness.

Snow drifted out of the cave.

It was spring.

And Gwen remembered the trance.

---

The caverns changed everything.

Egg chambers filled entire tunnels.

Frozen roots penetrated stone.

Insects carried food toward deeper chambers rather than eating it themselves.

This was not infestation.

It was infrastructure.

A kingdom.

Someone had reorganized the insects of Whisker Woods.

Gwen was moving through the outskirts of an empire.

That was also where she met Cedrick the Grunt.

Cedrick was a skeleton.

A skeleton with a spear.

A helmet.

And, unfortunately, employment.

"Halt!"

Gwen stopped.

Cedrick lowered the spear.

"State your business!"

"I'm looking for the thing freezing the forest."

"Unauthorized."

"Are you guarding it?"

Cedrick hesitated.

"I am guarding this hallway."

"Why?"

"Orders."

"From who?"

Another hesitation.

"Management."

Gwen drew her sword.

Cedrick sighed.

"Right."

The fight was considerably less majestic than Thairrott.

Cedrick fought hard.

Gwen fought better.

Eventually his spear flew across the cavern.

Cedrick hit the ground.

Gwen raised her sword.

"WAIT!"

She stopped.

"I surrender."

"You attacked me."

"I am a grunt. It's practically in the title."

Gwen narrowed her eyes.

Cedrick lifted both hands.

"I can be useful."

"How?"

"Charms."

Gwen paused.

"Charms?"

"Upgrades."

That got her attention.

Cedrick lowered his voice.

"Very powerful upgrades."

Gwen lowered the sword.

Cedrick smiled nervously.

"For a reasonable fee."

Gwen raised the sword again.

"A substantial fee."

"CEDRICK."

Eventually she spared him.

Not because he was useful.

Though he was.

And not because she trusted him.

She absolutely did not.

She spared him because beneath the armor, the orders and the terrible business instincts, Cedrick simply was not evil.

He was afraid.

Like half the creatures Gwen had encountered lately.

Later, Cedrick would exchange the helmet for a hood and become a strange recurring ally, improving Gwen's charms whenever she could afford his increasingly offensive prices.

But more importantly, he knew the tunnels.

And according to him, things deeper inside had changed recently.

The bugs had a Queen.

And the Queen had become cold.

---

Beyond Thairrott's cavern Gwen eventually crossed paths with Cedar and Lyra.

They did not welcome her.

In fact, they attacked her first.

Everyone had become suspicious.

The bugs were hostile.

Skeletons wandered farther than before.

Strange magic was appearing in animals.

People vanished along northern roads.

And Gwen had arrived carrying magical food from Tohm Kyatt.

After the misunderstanding—and several bruises—the three stopped fighting long enough to compare what they knew.

Cedar and Lyra brought Gwen to Oswin.

The old alchemist listened to Gwen describe Kap's pond.

The boar.

The lightning bug.

The frozen caverns.

And finally, the Fire Meal trance.

Oswin did not laugh.

He asked about Tohm.

Too many questions about Tohm.

Where had he learned the recipe?

Where did his magical meals come from?

Why had the corruption Gwen encountered carried a culinary signature similar to his magic?

Gwen became defensive.

Oswin noticed.

"Your mentor knows more than he says."

"Everyone knows more than they say."

"Not everyone has Tohm Kyatt's history."

"What history?"

Oswin smiled without humor.

"Ask him."

Gwen hated that answer almost as much as she hated Tohm's.

But Oswin gave her something useful.

The magic was spreading through consumption.

The creatures were not simply being cursed.

They were **eating** something.

Meals.

Fragments.

Residue.

Recipes.

Food was transforming them.

That meant whoever was behind this was using the same fundamental principle as Gwen.

Cooking.

---

Then Mu'Ramar's camp was destroyed.

Gwen found the tents shredded.

Supplies scattered.

Claw marks crossed the trees.

At first she assumed insects.

Then she saw the footprints.

Too large.

Too canine.

Night fell before she found the creature.

It found her first.

A towering beast crashed from the forest and attacked.

Gwen survived by inches.

Fire forced it back.

Snacks kept her moving.

Her charms bought her seconds.

But Gwen realized the thing was fighting strangely.

Not like an animal.

Like someone trapped inside one.

She stopped trying to kill it.

Instead she survived until sunrise.

When morning light crossed the trees, the beast collapsed.

And changed.

A person lay where it had fallen.

Terrified.

Exhausted.

Alive.

When Gwen finally got him talking, he told her what had happened.

Weeks earlier, someone had given him magical cookies.

Gwen closed her eyes.

"Who?"

"Cedrick."

There was a long silence.

Gwen returned to the cavern.

Cedrick saw her coming.

"Oh."

"You gave someone magical cookies?"

"They were promotional."

"He turned into a monster."

"There were side effects."

"YOU KNEW?"

"I knew afterward."

Cedrick's charm prices became substantially more negotiable that afternoon.

But the incident proved Oswin right.

The corruption was culinary.

And it was spreading farther than the Queen's hive.

Meals could empower.

Meals could heal.

Meals could transform.

Meals could destroy.

Someone had taken the same miracle Gwen was learning to use...

and removed every restraint.

---

The answer finally came with the ice.

A storm swallowed northern Whisker Woods overnight.

Not weather.

An expanding wall of winter.

Trees froze where they stood.

Streams stopped moving.

Entire insect colonies emerged coated in crystalline armor.

The Queen was no longer hiding underground.

She was claiming the forest.

The village elder finally told Gwen the oldest version of the story.

Long before Gwen.

Long before the current village.

The Whisken had once fought beside an Ant Queen against a gluttonous King.

The Queen had protected the balance of the forest.

Bugs cultivated soil.

Carried seeds.

Consumed decay.

The Whisken harvested the abundance that followed.

The Feast of Full Plates existed because once, two peoples had chosen sharing over hunger.

And now that same ancient line had become the greatest threat Whisker Woods had ever faced.

Gwen remembered the child.

Purple nightgown.

Faery doll.

A wounded insect.

Princess.

She went to Tohm.

"Who was she?"

Tohm said nothing.

Gwen placed a frozen shard of magical residue on the table.

"Who was the girl?"

His eyes fixed on it.

"Tohm."

He sat down.

And for once, the legendary chef seemed old.

"There was a princess."

Gwen waited.

"Lillia."

The name meant little to Gwen.

Not yet.

Tohm told her only part of the truth.

A human princess obsessed with faeries.

A royal family desperate to give their daughter magic.

A magical culinary experiment.

A recipe book.

Pages torn from it.

Pages scattered.

Recipes never meant to exist outside their proper context.

Dark Culinary Arts born from knowledge separated from restraint.

He did not tell Gwen everything.

He did not tell her about Tabby Island.

He did not tell her every part he had played.

But he told her enough.

One of those pages was in Whisker Woods.

And something had cooked from it.

---

Gwen entered the Ice Queen's hive carrying every lesson the forest had taught her.

Healing ales brewed from slime.

Quick snacks prepared from gathered ingredients.

Charm upgrades bought at insulting prices from Cedrick.

Fire Meal portions.

Knowledge from Oswin.

Directions from Cedar and Lyra.

Warnings from Bramrik, who still insisted the northern side technically belonged to him even while allowing refugees through his gate.

And a sword.

Always the sword.

The deeper Gwen went, the more the hive resembled her trance.

The same frozen roots.

The same egg chambers.

The same clicking echo.

Then she saw a carving along the wall.

A mark she remembered from the vision.

She stopped.

"This is it."

She had not dreamed the cavern.

She had remembered it.

Or someone had remembered it through her.

At the center of the hive stood the Queen.

Huge.

Ancient.

Terrible.

The ruler of the insects of Whisker Woods.

Ice covered her body like armor.

Ant-like limbs ended in frozen blades.

Her abdomen disappeared into the enormous nest behind her.

Thousands of creatures moved at her command.

Gwen looked at her.

And saw the scar.

A long fracture in the chitin along one side.

Old.

Healed decades ago.

The exact wound the little girl had been tending.

Gwen stopped breathing.

The shadowed creature in the trance.

The creature Lillia had saved.

It had been her.

The Ant Queen.

The Ice Queen.

---

The Queen screamed.

The hive answered.

Phase one began with the swarm.

Beetles poured into the chamber.

Dappleflies blackened the air.

Crayhusks emerged beneath the ice.

The Queen raised walls around Gwen, dividing the arena.

Ice spikes erupted from the ground.

Blizzard winds extinguished ordinary flame.

But not the Fire Meal.

Gwen ate.

Heat exploded through her.

Her sword ignited.

For the first time since entering the hive, the Queen recoiled.

Gwen burned paths through the swarm.

Melted barriers.

Shattered frozen limbs.

Every time the Queen summoned more insects, Gwen forced her closer to the center.

Fire against ice.

Meal against meal.

Until Gwen's final strike cracked the Queen's frozen shell.

The entire hive shook.

The Queen screamed.

Then became still.

For one second Gwen thought it was over.

Then the shell opened.

Something stepped out.

Tall.

Thin.

Almost elegant.

The Queen's second form no longer moved like an insect.

She moved like a dancer.

A grotesque ballerina carved from winter.

Her limbs extended into long crystalline shapes.

She spun across the arena.

Every movement created ice.

Every turn left frozen trails.

Every landing produced rings of spikes.

The battle acquired rhythm.

Spin.

Strike.

Pause.

Blizzard.

Leap.

Gwen could not simply attack.

She had to learn the dance.

She watched.

Waited.

Moved between the patterns.

Used snacks when the Fire Meal waned.

Drank ale when a blade caught her side.

Changed charms.

Adapted.

The Queen spun faster.

Gwen followed.

For an instant, beneath the shriek of wind, Gwen heard something impossible.

Humming.

The lullaby.

The same melody the little girl had sung in the cave.

The Queen remembered.

Somewhere beneath the corruption, she remembered Lillia.

Gwen hesitated.

The Queen struck.

Gwen flew across the ice.

Her sword skidded away.

The Queen approached.

Gwen looked at the creature.

"You remember her."

The Queen stopped.

Only for a heartbeat.

But it was enough.

Gwen reached for the last Fire Meal.

Ate.

Flame surged through her.

She called her sword back into her hand.

The Queen raised both arms.

The entire ceiling became ice.

Gwen charged.

The Queen spun.

Fire and frost collided.

And Gwen drove the blade through the center of the corrupted magic.

Everything stopped.

The Queen looked down.

A thin crack spread across her body.

Then another.

Then hundreds.

The ice shattered.

The Queen fell.

And Whisker Woods became quiet.

---

Something slipped from beneath the broken frost.

A page.

Gwen picked it up.

The parchment was impossibly warm on one side and freezing on the other.

Across the top was the name of a recipe.

**The Blizzard Meal.**

The moment Gwen touched it, the hive disappeared.

Another memory.

Clearer this time.

The same girl.

Older.

Lillia.

No longer the child in the purple nightgown.

Magic moved around her now.

Dangerous magic.

Behind her hovered torn pages covered in recipes.

Before her stood the Ant Queen.

Alive.

Uncorrupted.

Ancient.

Lillia held out a dish.

Frost curled from it.

The Queen hesitated.

Lillia smiled.

"You saved me."

Gwen saw fragments.

The childhood expedition.

Lillia becoming separated from guards.

The Ant Queen wounded.

The child hiding beside her.

Feeding her.

Tending her wounds.

The Queen later protecting the girl.

Guiding her from the woods.

A debt born between a lonely princess obsessed with magical creatures and an ancient creature most humans would have feared.

Years later Lillia had returned.

Changed.

Powerful.

Carrying a meal.

A gift.

A repayment.

The Ice Meal.

The Blizzard Meal.

The Queen accepted it because she remembered the child who had once shown her mercy.

And the meal corrupted her.

The memory vanished.

Gwen was back in the hive.

The dead Queen lay before her.

Gwen stared at the recipe page.

Lillia had not conquered the Queen.

She had not needed to.

The Queen had trusted her.

That was worse.

---

The thaw began slowly.

A drop of water fell from the ceiling.

Then another.

Outside, frozen branches cracked as they warmed.

Streams began moving.

The insects did not disappear.

They simply stopped marching.

Some fled.

Some returned underground.

Some wandered as though waking from a long nightmare.

Whisker Woods had not been cured.

Kap's pond was still damaged.

The corrupted roots remained.

The transformed creatures Gwen had encountered could not all be undone.

The recipe pages were still scattered across the world.

And somewhere beyond Whisker Woods was Princess Lillia.

But the storm was ending.

Gwen returned to the village carrying the Blizzard Meal page.

Bramrik opened his gate before she could knock.

He looked at the melting snow.

"My side survived."

Gwen smiled.

"Whisker Woods survived."

"My side especially."

"Still not yours."

He closed the gate.

Cedrick offered Gwen a commemorative discount on charms.

It was three percent.

She threatened him.

Ressa demanded a rematch once the roads reopened.

Juno immediately asked whether fighting an insect ballerina technically counted as dance training.

Kap returned to his pond.

Oswin continued watching Tohm.

And that evening, when the village gathered again around long tables, the Feast of Full Plates meant something different.

For generations, it had remembered an alliance between the Whisken and the insects of Whisker Woods.

Now it remembered its loss as well.

Gwen sat outside the tavern with a bowl in her hands.

Tohm joined her.

For a long time neither spoke.

Finally Gwen placed the Blizzard Meal page between them.

"You knew her."

Tohm looked at Lillia's handwriting.

"Yes."

"You knew what those pages could do."

A long silence.

"Yes."

"You knew more than you told me."

Another silence.

"Yes."

Gwen stared at him.

"One day, you're going to tell me everything."

Tohm looked toward the tavern.

Toward the village.

Toward the forest his choices had once helped shape in ways Gwen did not yet understand.

"One day," he said.

Gwen hated the answer.

But for tonight, she let him keep it.

She looked toward Whisker Woods.

The last snow was melting beneath the moon.

Somewhere in the darkness an ordinary beetle crawled across an ordinary branch.

No frost.

No command.

No Queen.

Gwen smiled.

Then she looked down at the recipe page.

At the name **Lillia** written faintly along its edge.

Whisker Woods had been only the beginning.

And Gwen was starting to understand something Tohm Kyatt should have taught her years ago.

A recipe was never just instructions.

A meal carried the intention of the person who made it.

Love could enter food.

Passion could enter food.

Memory could enter food.

And, in the wrong hands...

so could corruption.
