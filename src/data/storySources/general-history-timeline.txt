# General History Timeline

## Chapter 1—The Three-Hundred-Year War

For centuries, **Ovenhold** and the **Faery Realm** stood as two powerful but deeply different civilizations.

Ovenhold was a mortal kingdom shaped by hearths, ovens, labor, craft, agriculture, and cooked food. The Faery Realm was ancient, magical, proud, and closely bound to nature, spirit, and the unseen forces of the world.

### Two Kingdoms Divided

What began as distrust slowly hardened into hatred.

Disputes over land, resources, power, borders, and influence built up over time until conflict became unavoidable. Each kingdom came to fear the other. Pride made peace more difficult. Hunger made tempers worse. Old wounds were passed down, and before long the war had become larger than the people who first started it.

### Three Centuries of Hunger

The war lasted for **300 years**.

Generations were born into it. Fields were burned. Roads and trade collapsed. Forests became hunting grounds for soldiers instead of homes for wildlife. Food grew scarce, and feasts became a fading memory in much of the world.

The damage was not only physical. Hospitality itself began to die. The act of feeding others, welcoming strangers, and sharing a table became less and less common as both kingdoms gave themselves over to survival and vengeance.

### The Final Convergence

By the year **333**, the war had reached its breaking point.

The losses had grown unbearable. Both kingdoms were starving. Both had buried too many dead. Neither side had found a path toward peace, and neither side was willing to yield.

At last, the full might of **Ovenhold** and the **Faery Realm** gathered for one final confrontation.

In a vast open battlefield, the armies of both races stood facing one another with only a stretch of empty ground between them. The **Human King** stood at the front of Ovenhold's forces. The **Faery King** stood at the front of the Faery host. Behind them waited soldiers, nobles, hunters, knights, mages, faeries, and common folk who had all given everything they had left.

This battle was meant to decide everything.

No retreat. No compromise. No peace.

If it continued, both peoples might well have destroyed one another completely.

That was the moment the Tablemaker appeared.

## Chapter 2—The Tablemaker and the End of the War

A strange man walks into the space between two armies and changes history forever.

### The Prophet Seen in Years 330 to 333

By the time of the final battlefield in year **333**, some had already heard stories of a strange prophet-like figure known as **the Tablemaker**.

His first known appearance had been in the year **330**.

For three years, rumors of him spread from village to village and camp to camp. Some called him a madman. Some called him a prophet. Some claimed he called himself the true **Master Chef**, the one who understood that the world itself had been formed like a meal through the sacred balance of **Love, Taste, and Passion**.

Many ridiculed him. Many mocked him. Few took him seriously.

Even so, his name had spread far enough that when he stepped onto the final battlefield, some among both armies recognized him at once.

### The Man in the Middle of the Battlefield

As the armies prepared to collide, the Tablemaker walked into the empty space between them.

He did not carry a weapon.

He brought a table, ingredients, cooking tools, and the calm of a man who had already decided what he had come there to do.

The battlefield fell into a stunned silence.

With two armies watching him, the Tablemaker set his table in the very center of the field and began to cook.

### The Dance of the Meal

He worked with the focus and beauty of a choreographed performance.

Every slice was intentional. Every movement had a purpose. The turning of his wrist, the placement of each ingredient, the rhythm of his hands, the motion of blade, flame, steam, and pan all flowed together with the grace of a sacred dance.

He did not rush.

He did not hesitate.

He cooked as though the battlefield around him had vanished and only the meal remained.

The **Human King**, the **Faery King**, and the armies behind them stood in open shock. Some whispered that this was the man people had been talking about for three years. Others laughed at him. Some mocked him out loud and called him insane.

They jeered at his claims. They scoffed at his preaching. They called him a fool for stepping between two armies with a table instead of a sword.

He continued cooking without a word.

### The Glowing of the Tablemaker

As the meal took shape, something began to change.

A strange light rose from the food.

Then the Tablemaker's own body began to glow.

The glow grew brighter with each motion. His hands trembled, yet he never stopped. Pain began to show in him, but his cooking only became more intense, more precise, and more full of purpose.

The armies did not understand what they were seeing.

Fear turned quickly into violence.

Projectiles were loosed. Blades were driven forward. Attacks came from both sides. The Human host struck him. The Faery host struck him. Yet the Tablemaker did not turn to defend himself, and he did not cry out.

He simply kept cooking.

He cooked through the pain.

He cooked through the wounds.

He cooked with such overwhelming force of spirit, skill, and conviction that the light around him became impossible to look at directly.

He glowed brighter and brighter until, at last, his mortal body could bear no more.

The light burst from him.

The Tablemaker died there on the battlefield.

Many later claimed that in the instant after the burst, they saw a radiant form rising upward into the sky before it vanished. Whether all truly saw it, or whether some only believed they had, no one could ever say with certainty. But that belief endured, and it became part of the sacred memory of the event.

When the light faded, the battlefield had gone silent.

The Tablemaker was gone.

The meal remained.

### The First Taste

The food set upon the table looked unlike anything anyone had ever seen.

Its aroma spread across the battlefield and reached every soldier, every faery, every noble, and every starving survivor present. After centuries of famine and war, that scent struck them with a force stronger than fear.

The **Human King**, driven by hunger and astonishment, approached the table first.

The armies watched in total silence.

He took a bite.

At once, he fell to his knees.

The **Faery King**, shocked by the sight, stepped forward next. He looked at the kneeling Human King, ready for a moment that might have become murder only seconds earlier. But the smell of the meal and the sight of it before him proved impossible to resist.

He took a bite as well.

He too fell to the ground.

Then and there, both kings understood that they had tasted something beyond anything they had ever known. It was richer, deeper, more moving, and more alive than any meal in memory. It carried grief, mercy, beauty, warmth, welcome, and truth all at once.

It tasted like life returned to the dying.

It tasted like hunger answered at last.

It tasted like something no war had ever been able to produce and no kingdom could have forced into being.

The kings were not alone in this realization. As the meal was shared, the armies of both peoples came to understand the same thing.

The war ended there, in the year **333**, through the work and sacrifice of the Tablemaker.

### The Birth of the Triadic Faith

After the meal, **Ovenhold** and the **Faery Realm** joined together and swore to grow in peace.

They pledged to share their meals, their labor, and their future. From the Tablemaker's teaching, the shared faith of the two peoples took shape around the sacred Triad:

**Love. Taste. Passion.**

These three principles became the heart of the new faith and the foundation of the world that followed.

The Tablemaker's teaching was no longer a rumor mocked by soldiers. It became the truth by which kingdoms rebuilt themselves.

### The Everfeast and Food Essence

The Tablemaker's death also changed the understanding of cooking forever.

His final act revealed that food could carry divine force when made in perfect harmony with **Love**, **Taste**, and **Passion**. This force became known as **Food Essence**.

According to the faith that grew from his sacrifice, the Tablemaker did not simply perish. His spirit returned to **the Everfeast**, the heavenly culinary realm where all meals exist in their perfect form.

From that time onward, cooking was no longer seen as simple preparation alone. A truly great meal could carry power, memory, healing, transformation, and spiritual weight.

## Chapter 3—The Imperial Culinary Academy of Ovenhold

The peace that followed gave rise to an institution dedicated to preserving the Tablemaker's legacy.

### Why the Academy Was Founded

After the war, food took on a central place in society.

Meals were no longer treated as ordinary necessities. They became sacred expressions of culture, unity, healing, hospitality, and spiritual power. Great chefs were respected not only for their skill, but for their ability to shape lives and communities through Food Essence.

As this understanding spread, the united powers of **Ovenhold** and the **Faery Realm** recognized the need for a formal place of teaching.

### The Great School of Culinary Tradition

They founded the **Imperial Culinary Academy of Ovenhold**, based in **Ovenhold**, to train future generations in the art, discipline, and sacred responsibility of cooking.

The Academy became the greatest school of culinary learning in the known world.

There, students study ingredients, preparation, technique, magical meals, Food Essence, the philosophy of the Triad, and the role of food in both daily life and sacred tradition.

The purpose of the Academy is to preserve what the Tablemaker revealed and to ensure that future chefs continue striving toward meals worthy of being shared with the world.

### A Living Legacy

Over time, the Academy became one of the most respected institutions in society.

Its graduates include chefs, researchers, gatherers, culinary explorers, teachers, and masters of magical food. Some serve courts and cities. Others travel dangerous regions in search of rare ingredients. All inherit a craft shaped by the memory of the meal that ended the Three-Hundred-Year War.

The Academy stands as a monument to peace and as a living continuation of the Tablemaker's legacy.

## Chapter 4—General Note on Other Faiths

Although the **Triadic Faith** is the central shared faith of **Ovenhold** and the **Faery Realm**, it is not the only religion in the world.

### The Masil Cult

The **Masil Cult** exists as its own independent religion and holds strong influence over the **Eastern Human Kingdom**. In that region, it has become dominant, and many of the local people have been subjugated under its rule.

### The Whiskin Folk

The **Whiskin Folk** traditionally followed an older earth-centered religion tied to the land, the ground, and the natural world.

However, **Whiskin Village** in **Whisker Woods** has since converted to the **Triadic Faith** through the influence of **Tom**.

In the current era, **Tom** is part of the Triadic Faith, while **Gwen** is not yet. Her relationship to that faith develops gradually over the course of the game.

### Elves and Dwarves

The **Elves** and the **Dwarves** each maintain their own distinct faiths as well. Their beliefs are separate from both the Triadic Faith and the Masil Cult, and they remain part of the broader religious landscape of the world.
